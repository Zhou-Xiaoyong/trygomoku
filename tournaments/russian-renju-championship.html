Russian Renju Championship — Official Rules

The Чемпионат России по рэндзю (Russian Renju Championship) is the premier national Renju competition in Russia, organized by the Russian Renju Federation. Below are the core rules used in the championship, which this practice board enforces automatically.

1

Board & Stones

Played on a 15×15 grid. One player uses black stones, the other white stones. Black always moves first.

2

Winning Condition

The first player to form an unbroken line of five stones horizontally, vertically, or diagonally wins. White may also win with five or more (overline allowed for White). Black must form exactly five — six or more is a forbidden move.

3

Black Forbidden Moves (黑棋禁手)

To balance Black's first-move advantage, Black is forbidden from creating three patterns. If Black plays a forbidden move, Black loses immediately:

  • 三三禁手 (Double Three) — placing a stone that simultaneously creates two or more "live threes" (活三).
  • 四四禁手 (Double Four) — placing a stone that simultaneously creates two or more "fours" (活四 or 冲四).
  • 長連禁手 (Overline) — placing a stone that forms six or more consecutive stones.

Black can only win by a 四三 (Four-Three) — one four and one three from the same move.

4

Draw

If the entire board fills up without either player forming five in a row, the game is a draw.

Note on opening rules: Russian Renju follows RIF standard rules. Russia was one of the three founding members of RIF (alongside Japan and Sweden) in 1988. Russian players have won multiple world championship titles under the RIF system. The core competitive rules above — 15×15 board and Black forbidden moves — apply across all championship groups and are what this practice board enforces.

About the Russian Renju Championship

The Чемпионат России по рэндзю (Russian Renju Championship) is Russia's premier national Renju competition, held annually by the Russian Renju Federation. Russia's Renju tradition dates back to the Soviet era, and the USSR was one of the three founding members of the RIF (Renju International Federation) in 1988, alongside Japan and Sweden.

World Championship Legacy

Russian players have dominated international Renju for decades. World champions from Russia include Alexander Chingin, Konstantin Chernetsov, and Pavel Salnikov, each of whom has won multiple world championship titles. Russia continues to host its national championship annually as a key selection event for the Russian national team ahead of World Renju Championships.

Significance of the 禁手 Rules

The forbidden-move (禁手) rules are the defining feature of competitive Renju in Russia. Because Gomoku is a solved game where Black (first player) can force a win with perfect play, the 禁手 rules are essential for fair competition. They force Black to play more carefully and strategically, making the game balanced and interesting at the highest level.

Tips for Practicing with Russian Championship Rules

Learn the Forbidden Patterns

  • Spend time studying what a 三三 (double three) looks like. Many beginners lose accidentally to this rule.
  • A 四四 (double four) is also easy to stumble into when building attacks.
  • Remember that an overline (長連) of 6+ stones counts as a loss for Black, not a win.

Play as Black to Master 禁手

  • Practice as Black to learn which moves are forbidden. The board will reject illegal moves automatically.
  • Understanding the restrictions will make you a stronger White player too — you can deliberately steer Black into 禁手 positions.
  • Black must win with a 四三 (four-three). Practice setting up this winning combination.

Use the AI Sparring Partner

  • Start on Medium difficulty to get used to the forbidden-move rules.
  • Move up to Hard or Master once you're comfortable avoiding 禁手.
  • Use the Hint button when you're unsure whether a move is legal or strategically sound.

FAQ — Russian Renju Championship Rules

What are the forbidden moves (禁手) in Russian Renju rules?

Black has three types of forbidden moves: 三三禁手 (double three) — creating two or more live threes in one move; 四四禁手 (double four) — creating two or more fours in one move; and 長連禁手 (overline) — forming six or more consecutive stones. If Black makes any of these moves, Black loses the game.

Does White also have forbidden moves?

No. White has no forbidden moves. White can win with five, six, or more consecutive stones. The restrictions apply only to Black to compensate for the first-move advantage.

Was Russia a founding member of RIF?

Yes. The USSR (represented by Russian Renju players) was one of the three founding members of the Renju International Federation (RIF) in 1988, alongside Japan and Sweden. Russia has remained a leading Renju nation ever since.

Who are the famous Russian Renju world champions?

Notable Russian world champions include Alexander Chingin, Konstantin Chernetsov, and Pavel Salnikov. Each has won multiple world championship titles, cementing Russia's place as one of the strongest Renju nations in the world.

Are Russian Renju rules the same as Japanese Renju?

Russian Renju follows the RIF standard, sharing the same core forbidden-move system (三三, 四四, 長連) as Japanese Renju. Both are designed to balance Black's first-move advantage. The main differences are in the opening rules — Russian championship games typically use the RIF Swap2 opening system. On this practice board, the rule set is labeled "禁手 (Renju Rules)" and enforces the standard forbidden moves.

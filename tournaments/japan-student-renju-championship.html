Japan Student Renju Championship — Rules

The 全日本学生連珠選手権 (Japan Student Renju Championship) is Japan's premier student Renju competition, organized by the Japan Renju Federation. Below are the core rules used in the competitive divisions, which this practice board enforces automatically.

1

Board & Stones

Played on a 15×15 grid. One player uses black stones, the other white stones. Black always moves first.

2

Winning Condition

The first player to form an unbroken line of five stones horizontally, vertically, or diagonally wins. White may also win with five or more (overline allowed for White). Black must form exactly five — six or more is a forbidden move.

3

Black Forbidden Moves (黒棋禁手)

To balance Black's first-move advantage, Black is forbidden from creating three patterns. If Black plays a forbidden move, Black loses immediately:

  • 三三禁手 (Double Three) — placing a stone that simultaneously creates two or more "live threes" (活三).
  • 四四禁手 (Double Four) — placing a stone that simultaneously creates two or more "fours" (活四 or 冲四).
  • 長連禁手 (Overline) — placing a stone that forms six or more consecutive stones.

Black can only win by a 四三 (Four-Three) — one four and one three from the same move.

4

Student Divisions

The championship features divisions for elementary school (小学生), middle school (中学生), and high school (高校生) students. Each division lets students compete against peers of similar age and experience.

Note on the rule system: The student championship follows standard Japanese Renju rules. Some younger divisions may use simplified rules, but the competitive divisions use the full 禁手 rules (三三, 四四, 長連). This practice board enforces the full 禁手 rules so students can train against the competitive standard.

About the Japan Student Renju Championship

The 全日本学生連珠選手権 (Japan Student Renju Championship) is Japan's premier student Renju competition. It is organized by the Japan Renju Federation and held annually, providing a national stage for young Renju talent.

Promoting Renju Among Young Players

The championship exists to promote Renju among young players. By offering divisions for elementary, middle, and high school students, it gives players a clear path from their first games to competitive play against peers nationwide.

Connection to the Meijinsen

Strong student performers often go on to compete in the All Japan Renju Meijinsen, Japan's most prestigious Renju title. The student championship is therefore an important stepping stone toward the highest levels of Japanese Renju.

Tips for Student Renju Practice

Learn the Forbidden Patterns

  • Spend time studying what a 三三 (double three) looks like. Many students lose accidentally to this rule.
  • A 四四 (double four) is also easy to stumble into when building attacks.
  • Remember that a 長連 (overline) of 6+ stones counts as a loss for Black, not a win.

Play as Black to Master 禁手

  • Practice as Black to learn which moves are forbidden. The board will reject illegal moves automatically.
  • Understanding the restrictions will make you a stronger White player too — you can deliberately steer Black into 禁手 positions.
  • Black must win with a 四三 (four-three). Practice setting up this winning combination.

Train for Your Division

  • Start on Medium difficulty to get used to the forbidden-move rules used in the competitive divisions.
  • Move up to Hard or Master once you're comfortable avoiding 禁手.
  • Use the Hint button when you're unsure whether a move is legal or strategically sound.

FAQ — Japan Student Renju Championship Rules

What are the forbidden moves (禁手) in Japanese Renju?

Black has three types of forbidden moves: 三三禁手 (double three) — creating two or more live threes in one move; 四四禁手 (double four) — creating two or more fours in one move; and 長連禁手 (overline) — forming six or more consecutive stones. If Black makes any of these moves, Black loses the game.

Does White also have forbidden moves?

No. White has no forbidden moves. White can win with five, six, or more consecutive stones. The restrictions apply only to Black to compensate for the first-move advantage.

What student divisions does the championship have?

The championship features divisions for elementary school (小学生), middle school (中学生), and high school (高校生) students. Each division lets students compete against peers of similar age and experience.

Can Black win with six or more in a row?

No. In Renju rules, Black can only win with exactly five consecutive stones. Six or more is a 長連禁手 (overline) and results in an immediate loss for Black. White, however, can win with five or more.

Is the student championship connected to the Meijinsen?

Yes. Strong student performers often go on to compete in the All Japan Renju Meijinsen, Japan's most prestigious Renju title. The student championship is therefore an important stepping stone toward the highest levels of Japanese Renju.

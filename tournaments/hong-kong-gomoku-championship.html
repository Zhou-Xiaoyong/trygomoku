Hong Kong Gomoku Championship — Official Rules

The 香港五子棋錦標賽 (Hong Kong Gomoku Championship) is the flagship Gomoku competition in Hong Kong, organized by the Hong Kong Gomoku Association. Below are the core rules used in the championship, which this practice board enforces automatically.

1

Board & Stones

Played on a 15×15 grid. One player uses black stones, the other white stones. Black always moves first.

2

Winning Condition

The first player to form an unbroken line of five stones horizontally, vertically, or diagonally wins. White may also win with five or more (overline allowed for White). Black must form exactly five — six or more is a forbidden move.

3

Black Forbidden Moves (黑棋禁手)

To balance Black's first-move advantage, Black is forbidden from creating three patterns. If Black plays a forbidden move, Black loses immediately:

  • 三三禁手 (Double Three) — placing a stone that simultaneously creates two or more "live threes" (活三).
  • 四四禁手 (Double Four) — placing a stone that simultaneously creates two or more "fours" (活四 or 冲四).
  • 长连禁手 (Overline) — placing a stone that forms six or more consecutive stones.

Black can only win by a 四三 (Four-Three) — one four and one three from the same move.

4

Draw

If the entire board fills up without either player forming five in a row, the game is a draw.

Note on the rule system: Hong Kong follows the Chinese Gomoku competition rules (中国五子棋竞赛规则) with forbidden moves for Black. Hong Kong players frequently participate in mainland China tournaments, so the rules are kept fully compatible with the national standard. About the Hong Kong Gomoku Championship

The 香港五子棋錦標賽 (Hong Kong Gomoku Championship) is Hong Kong's premier Gomoku competition. Hong Kong has a vibrant Gomoku scene, with active clubs and a steady stream of school-level competitions feeding into the championship.

Connection to Mainland China Tournaments

Because Hong Kong follows the same Chinese Gomoku competition rules as the mainland, top Hong Kong players frequently participate in mainland China tournaments, including the China National Gomoku Championship. This shared rule set makes cross-border competition seamless.

School Competitions

Gomoku is popular in Hong Kong schools, with inter-school competitions building a pipeline of young talent into the championship. The school events use the same 15×15 board and Black forbidden-move rules, so practicing here prepares students for both school and championship play.

Tips for Practicing with Hong Kong Rules

Learn the Forbidden Patterns

  • Spend time studying what a 三三 (double three) looks like. Many beginners lose accidentally to this rule.
  • A 四四 (double four) is also easy to stumble into when building attacks.
  • Remember that an overline (长连) of 6+ stones counts as a loss for Black, not a win.

Play as Black to Master 禁手

  • Practice as Black to learn which moves are forbidden. The board will reject illegal moves automatically.
  • Understanding the restrictions will make you a stronger White player too — you can deliberately steer Black into 禁手 positions.
  • Black must win with a 四三 (four-three). Practice setting up this winning combination.

Use the AI Sparring Partner

  • Start on Medium difficulty to get used to the forbidden-move rules.
  • Move up to Hard or Master once you're comfortable avoiding 禁手.
  • Use the Hint button when you're unsure whether a move is legal or strategically sound.

FAQ — Hong Kong Gomoku Championship Rules

What rules does the Hong Kong Gomoku Championship use?

The Hong Kong Gomoku Championship uses the Chinese Gomoku competition rules (中国五子棋竞赛规则), played on a 15×15 board with Black forbidden moves (三三, 四四, 长连). Hong Kong keeps its rules fully compatible with the mainland national standard so that players can compete cross-border.

What are the forbidden moves (禁手) for Black?

Black has three types of forbidden moves: 三三禁手 (double three) — creating two or more live threes in one move; 四四禁手 (double four) — creating two or more fours in one move; and 长连禁手 (overline) — forming six or more consecutive stones. If Black makes any of these moves, Black loses the game.

Can Black win with six or more in a row?

No. In the Hong Kong rules, Black can only win with exactly five consecutive stones. Six or more is a 长连禁手 (overline) and results in an immediate loss for Black. White, however, can win with five or more.

Can Hong Kong players join mainland China tournaments?

Yes. Because Hong Kong uses the same Chinese Gomoku competition rules as the mainland, top Hong Kong players frequently participate in mainland China tournaments, including the China National Gomoku Championship. The shared rule set makes cross-border competition seamless.

Are Hong Kong school competitions the same as the championship?

Hong Kong school competitions use the same 15×15 board and Black forbidden-move rules as the championship, so practicing here prepares students for both school and championship play. The championship level simply features stronger opposition and stricter pairing systems.

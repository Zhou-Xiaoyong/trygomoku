Half the Table Is Shopping. Who Plays Black Friday Gomoku?

2026-11-27 8 min read

Nine-forty on Friday morning. Two cars are already at the outlet mall, one adult has the laptop open on three tabs of deals, and an eight-year-old is standing at the table holding a tablet that nobody else wants.

Play two-player, on nine lines, five minutes a game. That is the whole answer. A game that short can end while someone is still deciding whether the second round of sales is worth the drive.

The cast changes every hour

A dinner party assumes a fixed group for a couple of hours. Friday does not work that way. Two people leave at ten, one comes back at one, another goes out at two because a cousin texted about a different store. The eight-year-old is there the entire time, waiting for a partner.

Any format that needs a set headcount dies at the first "I'll be back in forty minutes." Brackets, ladders, round robins: every one of them expects the same four names on the sheet at the end that were there at the start. Build the day around two players and treat each extra person as a bonus, not a requirement. The family-night format is worth settling on Friday morning, before the first car leaves, rather than rebuilding it after every departure.

Nine lines, not fifteen

A 9×9 board has 81 intersections. A 15×15 board has 225. Between two people who play a few times a year, fifteen lines is a twenty-five minute game, and it does not survive a single interruption. Nine lines is five.

The smaller board also changes what the game is about. On nine lines the whole fives problem shows up early: a double-three forms somewhere around move twelve, and by move fifteen somebody is already defending two directions at once. That is the part of gomoku worth teaching a guest, and on a big board you would still be shuffling stones in the corners when the conversation moves on.

If the board has to be set up from nothing, the first stone goes on the centre intersection, and the setup page covers the rest of the sequence. It takes about thirty seconds and saves the argument about who touched the board first.

What happens when a player walks off mid-game

One rule: nobody waits. Finish the game in progress with whoever is still sitting there, and the person who arrives takes the loser's colour.

Two things about this board make that work without a scoresheet. The scoreboard counts by colour, not by name, and it does not clear between games. A rotating table therefore builds one running total across the whole afternoon, which is exactly what you want when the people keep changing. It also produces a strange moment around four o'clock, when the numbers read 7 to 5 instead of 1 to 0, and someone assumes the board is broken.

The other thing to know in advance: the colour button is dead in two-player mode, and every reset opens with black again. You cannot rotate the first-move edge with a toggle. Swap seats instead, and let the person who has been losing take the black side of the tablet.

Let the kid who did not go shopping play white

Turn renju on, put the adult on black, and give the child white. Black's double-three, double-four and overline get refused outright: the stone snaps back, a short message appears for about two and a half seconds, and it is still black's turn. That single toggle removes most of the cheap wins an adult collects against an eight-year-old, because the standard adult trick of stacking two open threes in one move is no longer legal.

White has no restrictions at all, so the child can play the same careless attacking game and only lose on the board, not on a rule they have not learned yet. Five sentences cover what they need to know, and the kid version of the rules is written to be read out loud at the table rather than handed over.

If it is already eight at night

The shoppers are back, the bags are stacked by the door, and there is an hour left before someone starts yawning. Nine lines, renju off, first to two wins, and the loser hands the tablet to whoever has not played all day. Five minutes a game, fifteen for the match, done before the room loses interest.

Open the board in the browser and tap versus friend. No download, no account, nothing to install on a tablet that belongs to someone else.

The one number to report at the end

Somebody will ask who won. Answer by colour, not by name. The people changed five times today; the two colours did not. Black and white are the only two entrants that were present from the first game to the last.

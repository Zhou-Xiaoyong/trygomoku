How Many Pieces Do You Need to Win at Gomoku?

2026-08-26 8 min read

The Short Answer: Five Stones in a Row

If you have ever asked how many pieces to win gomoku, the answer is refreshingly simple: five. You win the moment you place the fifth stone of an unbroken line, horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. Four is close, but four is not a win.

Does It Matter Who Moves First?

No. Whether you play Black, who moves first, or White, who replies, the winning condition is identical: line up five of your own stones. The first-player advantage is a balance question, not a rules difference.

Why Four Is Not a Win: The Most Common Mix-Up

Beginners often celebrate reaching four in a row, assuming the game is over. It is not. Your opponent still gets a turn and can block the open end. That is why a so-called open four, a line of four with both ends free, is so powerful: it cannot be blocked in a single move. But it still takes the fifth stone to actually win. Watch out for the common gomoku mistakes that come from over-celebrating four.

What About Six in a Row?

In casual free-style play, a line of six or more usually still counts as a win. Under strict tournament Renju rules, however, a line longer than five, an overline, does not win: it is a forbidden over-reach. For everyday play, just aim for exactly five and you will never worry about the edge cases. The basics are covered in what gomoku is and how you win.

Build Toward Five: A Quick Practice Habit

The fastest way to internalize that five wins is to play. Open near the center, as described in how to set up the gomoku board, then focus each move on extending your longest line while capping your opponent's. When you feel the rhythm, play Gomoku free in your browser and try to force your first five-in-a-row.
